WHAT’S MMX

By returning 10g of material from the Martian moon, Phobos,
and conducting observations of the moons,
the origin of the Mars system
and the pathways for the emergence of habitability
and life can be explored.
This is a world-first mission led by JAXA.

The Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission will primarily focus on Phobos, a Martian moon that has never been explored. The mission will observe the topography, internal structure, composition, and gravity of the moon, and return samples of regolith.

How did the Martian moons form? How did Mars and our Solar System evolve? Through detailed observations and analysis of the samples obtained from Phobos, this mission seeks to clarify the formation of the Martian sphere and the Solar System, explore how life began, and pave the way for future deep-space exploration.

Launch is scheduled in autumn 2026, with the return to Earth targeted for 2031.

MISSION

MISSION 01

Planetary Science

Exploring the origin of the Martian moons and unveiling the evolutionary history of the Mars system

©DLR 2024

  1. 01Uncover the origin of the Martian moons and investigate how the planetary formation process unfolded in the transition region between the inner and outer Solar System, including mechanisms that transported key materials–such as water and organic matter–necessary for the emergency and habitability of life on terrestrial planets.
  2. 02Clarify the mechanisms driving change in the Martian sphere from the vantage point of its moons, providing new insights into the evolutionary history of the Mars system.

MISSION 02

Acquisition of Exploration Technology

Laying the foundations for future space exploration

©DLR 2024

  1. 01Develop technology for enabling round-trip missions to the Martian sphere and access to planetary moons.
  2. 02Develop technology for reaching the surface of the Martian moons, supporting a sustained stay, and enabling advanced sampling on celestial bodies.
  3. 03Develop communication technology optimised for reliable operations with ground stations during deep space exploration.

PROJECT MANAGER

Martian Moons Exploration Project Team
Project Manager

KAWAKATSU Yasuhiro

I am Kawakatsu Yasuhiro, Project Manager for the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission

The Martian moon Phobos remains an unexplored frontier for humankind. By bringing home samples from Phobos, we hope to explore how the Martian moons were formed, how the Mars sphere evolved, and how the water and organic materials found on the terrestrial planets originated. We will also develop exploration technology that will be necessary for human exploration in the future.

This exploration project began almost ten years ago. From the engineering perspective, this is an ambitious plan and the mission has gained much international attention. With researchers and engineers from Japan and overseas working together to overcome the numerous challenges, we are finally approaching launch in JFY 2026. We will be very grateful for your support over the five years from launch until our return to Earth.

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR

MMX Project Principal Investigator

KURAMOTO Kiyoshi

In ancient times, Mars was a world shrouded in a thick atmosphere. Rivers flowed across the planet's landscape into vast oceans. How did Mars acquire such a primordial, water-rich environment where life could conceivably have existed? The key to this mystery lies in the Martian moons, which closely resemble carbonaceous asteroids—the very celestial bodies capable of carrying the ingredients for an atmosphere and water.

Through the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission, we aim to unravel this question and shed light on the origin of the moons themselves. The mission will also investigate the processes that led to Mars losing its atmosphere and water, through atmospheric observation and analysing the material ejected from Martian craters.

MMX promises to reveal a great deal about how the Earth—the neighbour to Mars—became a planet capable of sustaining life, and how our planet has managed to remain so.

SPACECRAFT

The MMX spacecraft consists of three modules: the propulsion module, the exploration module, and the return module. It will carry out a five-year mission to the Martian moons and back to Earth.

The design leverages its versatility to support landing operations on the Martian moon, long-distance round-trip travel between Earth and the Mars sphere, and sample collection.

MISSION INSTRUMENTS

To unravel the mysteries of the Martian moons, we must decide what to observe with which instrument, and which samples to collect. The MMX spacecraft will use thirteen scientific mission instruments to explore the moons, and the landing site will be selected based on remote-sensing data. The goal is to return more than 10g of material to Earth.
